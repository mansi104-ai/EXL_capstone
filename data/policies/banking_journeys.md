# Northbank — Journey Playbook (Consumer Duty & Product Rules)

This playbook is organised by **banking journey** rather than by vulnerability
driver. It exists because the two previous documents answer "this customer is
vulnerable — now what?", while a handler on a live call is usually asking the
opposite question: "this is a collections call / a scam report / an estate —
what does the rulebook require of me here, and what changes because this customer
is vulnerable?"

Every clause names the obligation it derives from. Detection remains advisory;
every adaptation offered must be recorded. All figures and contact details are
illustrative.

---

## Driver: Resilience

### VP-J1 — Forbearance and due consideration (CONC 7.3)
Journeys: forbearance_request, arrears_collections, affordability_shock
Products: credit_card, personal_loan, overdraft, car_finance
Offer: I can reduce your payments to something you can actually manage
Offer: I can freeze the interest and charges while we sort out a plan
Offer: I can accept a smaller payment for now, with no pressure to catch up quickly
Where a customer is in or approaching arrears on a regulated credit agreement,
treat the customer with forbearance and due consideration. Consider, and offer
where suitable: suspending, reducing or deferring payments; accepting token
payments for a reasonable period; freezing or reducing interest and charges; and
agreeing a sustainable repayment arrangement based on what the customer can
actually afford after essentials. Do not press for a payment the customer has
said they cannot make, and do not treat a request for help as a default event.
Record the option offered, the option accepted, and the review date.

### VP-J2 — Breathing Space and free debt advice
Journeys: forbearance_request, arrears_collections, affordability_shock
Offer: I can pause interest, fees and any contact for sixty days while you get advice
Offer: I can give you the number for StepChange — free, independent debt advice
Where a customer is engaging with a debt adviser, or would benefit from doing so,
explain the Debt Respite Scheme (Breathing Space): a moratorium during which
interest, fees and enforcement action are paused. Signpost free debt advice —
StepChange, National Debtline, Citizens Advice — as free and independent, and
never present a fee-charging alternative as equivalent. If a Breathing Space
moratorium is already in force, stop all collections contact immediately.

### VP-J3 — Income and expenditure without pressure
Journeys: forbearance_request, affordability_shock, arrears_collections
Offer: We can do this over more than one call if that's easier
Offer: I can take the figures from your debt adviser instead, if you'd prefer
When assessing what a customer can afford, use a standard income-and-expenditure
review and allow essential costs — housing, energy, council tax, food, transport,
childcare, and disability-related costs — before any credit repayment. Offer to
complete it over more than one call, or to accept a figure from a debt adviser
instead. Never ask a customer to justify essential spending, and never propose a
figure that leaves essentials unmet.

### VP-J4 — Priority of essentials over our debt (Consumer Duty PRIN 2A.6)
Journeys: arrears_collections, affordability_shock, forbearance_request
Offer: Please pay your rent, energy and food first — we can wait
Offer: I can stop the letters and calls while we get this sorted
Where a customer indicates they are choosing between an essential bill and a
payment to us, our debt is non-priority. Say so explicitly, pause collections
activity, and move the conversation to support. Avoiding foreseeable harm takes
precedence over recovering the balance on this call.

## Driver: Life events

### VP-J5 — Bereavement: a single point of contact
Journeys: bereavement_estate, third_party_access
Products: current_account, savings, mortgage, personal_loan, credit_card
Offer: I can be your single point of contact, so you only have to tell me once
Offer: I can stop the interest, charges and letters from today
Offer: I only need to see the death certificate once
On notification of a death, open a bereavement case with one named point of
contact so the customer never repeats the disclosure. Suspend interest, charges,
arrears activity and marketing on the deceased's accounts from the date of
notification. Accept a death certificate copy once, and set out clearly which
documents are needed and which are not. Offer to deal with a representative and
allow the customer to set the pace — there is no deadline the customer must meet
on this call.

### VP-J6 — Estate administration and joint holdings
Journeys: bereavement_estate
Products: mortgage, personal_loan, savings, current_account
Offer: I can explain plainly what passes to you and what doesn't
Offer: I can pause the payments while everything is settled, and confirm that in writing
Where accounts are held jointly, explain plainly what passes to the survivor and
what forms part of the estate, without requiring legal knowledge. Do not pursue
the survivor for the deceased's sole debts. Where a mortgage or loan is affected,
offer a payment concession while the estate is settled and confirm it in writing.
Signpost probate support and, where the customer is managing alone, bereavement
support organisations.

## Driver: Capability

### VP-J7 — Authorised push payment (APP) scams
Journeys: fraud_scam
Products: current_account, savings
Offer: I can stop any further payments going out right now
Offer: I can try to get the money back from the other bank
Offer: I can explain how and when you'd get your money back
Where a customer reports that they were deceived into authorising a payment,
treat it as an APP scam claim, not a customer error. Never suggest moving money
to a "safe account" — that instruction is the scam. Take the report without
attributing blame, stop further payments, attempt recall, and explain the
reimbursement process and its timescales. Assess whether the customer's
circumstances made them more susceptible and record it. Offer a fraud-support
referral and, where the loss is significant, a wellbeing check.

### VP-J8 — Third-party mandates, capacity and power of attorney
Journeys: third_party_access, bereavement_estate
Offer: I can set up formal permission so you can help properly and safely
Offer: I'd still like to speak with you directly wherever you'd like me to
Where someone else operates the account, confirm the basis — a registered lasting
power of attorney, a court-appointed deputy, a third-party mandate, or informal
help. Under the Mental Capacity Act 2005, assume capacity unless there is
evidence otherwise, assess decision by decision, and never treat a diagnosis as
proof of incapacity. Where help is informal, offer a formal arrangement that
protects the customer, and speak to the customer directly wherever they wish.

## Driver: Health

### VP-J9 — Gambling harm: tools before credit
Journeys: gambling_harm, product_sale
Products: current_account, credit_card, overdraft
Offer: I can put a block on gambling payments today
Offer: I can set a daily spending limit, and stop any credit offers reaching you
Offer: I can give you the number for GamCare — that's 0808 8020 133
Where a customer indicates gambling harm, offer the practical controls first: a
gambling block on the card, spending and ATM limits, a cooling-off period on
credit applications, and removal from credit marketing. Do not offer additional
credit, a limit increase, or a consolidation loan, and decline an inbound request
for one with an explanation and an offer of support instead. Signpost GamCare
(0808 8020 133) and GAMSTOP without judgement.

### VP-J10 — Serious illness affecting a credit agreement
Journeys: forbearance_request, affordability_shock, arrears_collections
Products: mortgage, personal_loan, credit_card
Offer: I can reduce your payments while you're unwell
Offer: I can check whether your account has cover that would help with this
Where serious illness has reduced a customer's income or capacity to manage a
credit agreement, offer a payment concession while their situation settles,
without requiring medical evidence beyond a single confirmation. Check whether a
payment protection or critical illness benefit exists on the account, and offer
to check entitlement to statutory support. Do not require the customer to repeat
the diagnosis to another team.

## Cross-cutting: sales, suitability and support

### VP-J11 — Do not sell into vulnerability (Consumer Duty PRIN 2A.3)
Journeys: product_sale, affordability_shock, gambling_harm
Offer: I won't try to sell you anything today
Offer: I can look properly at whether that's right for you before anything happens
Where a vulnerability signal is present, stop any sales or upgrade process for
this contact. Do not present further borrowing as a solution to unaffordability.
If the customer initiates a product request, complete an affordability and
suitability check that accounts for their circumstances, and record the reason
for proceeding or declining.

### VP-J12 — Complaints and vulnerability (DISP 1)
Journeys: complaint
Offer: I can look after this myself so you're not passed around
Offer: I can explain how to take this further if you're not happy with the outcome
Where a customer complains and a vulnerability signal is present, prioritise the
case, assign a named handler, and adapt the channel and timescales to the
customer's needs. Explain the Financial Ombudsman Service route in plain language
and without discouragement. Consider whether distress and inconvenience should be
recognised in the outcome.

### VP-J13 — Recording, consent and re-contact
Journeys: general_servicing, forbearance_request, bereavement_estate, fraud_scam
Offer: I can note this so the next person you speak to already knows
Offer: I'll only share this with the team who need it — is that alright?
Record the vulnerability signal, the adaptation offered, and the customer's
response, using the customer's own words where possible. Explain what will be
recorded and why, and ask before sharing it beyond the team that needs it. Set a
review date so a temporary adaptation is revisited rather than left to lapse, and
make sure the next handler sees it so the customer never has to explain twice.
